# 2026.08.06 Update

## What We Got Wrong

- Debian's General Resolution on LLM usage was not withdrawn. New vote with three competing proposals runs until 2026-08-28.
- The Assisted-by commit trailer is not a de facto standard. Kubernetes bans AI trailers and requires disclosure in the PR description instead. They state that "it's about maintaining clear accountability" and including trailers could diminish human accountability.
- The claim that data privacy risk "is not as present during software development" was wrong. Coding agents now account for most reported agentic AI security incidents.
- Our energy appendix overstated per-query usage. Measured datacenter figures are 0.3 Wh for typical queries and 4 Wh for long reasoning queries.
- METR has revised its early-2025 developer slowdown finding. Its 2026 follow-up reports weak evidence of a speedup.

## What Changed Out There

- LLM chat logs may be disclosed upon court order, having implications for security vulnerability disclosure.
- AI code security pass rates stayed near 55% for two years despite large capability gains.
- Stack Overflow questions fell 78% year on year, showing accelerating erosion of the shared knowledge commons.
- The US Copyright Office confirmed wholly AI-generated output is not copyrightable and prompting alone is not authorship.
- Amnesty International concluded generative AI built on unlawful scraping is incompatible with international human rights law.
- Research showed AI disclosure erodes trust, even when it is honest.
- The Bank for International Settlements named an AI investment bust and circular financing as risks to the global financial system.
- AI datacenter demand drove severe memory price rises, with DRAM contract prices up around 60% in a single quarter.
- Fully open models became viable; OLMo 3 releases weights, training data, and code under Apache 2.0.
- Surveys found 93% of aid workers have used AI but only 22% work under a formal AI policy.

## What We Changed

- Harm reduction framing added: no shaming, no forced AI usage, and disclosure paired with an explicit no-penalty norm.
- Security section now covers more possible attack vectors.
- Environmental section now weighs per-prompt energy against the wider effects of demand, adds collective action as a mitigation, and the appendix notes water usage.
- Labour section substantiated with Fairwork, SOMO, Amnesty, and investigative reporting.
- Copyright section updated with what the current legal position means in practice.
- Digital divide section reframed around community consultation and sovereignty rather than benevolence.
- New economic and financial risk section covering pricing volatility, bubble risk, and hardware costs.
- More evidence for maintainer review burden claims.
- Project policy list updated with GeoServer and Kubernetes entries, the Debian vote, and links to living policy trackers.
- Open models page now defines open-weight versus fully open and lists first recommendations.
- Repo checklist warns against AI detector tools, which misclassify non-native English writers.
- Add adapted AI assistance scale (levels 0-5), replacing the yes/no disclosure checkbox in the PR template. Level 5 covers work no human has read, so that case can be declared honestly rather than hidden; it is a declaration for maintainers to act on, not an accepted level.
